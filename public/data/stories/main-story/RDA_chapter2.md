At exactly 8:00 AM, the email landed in every inbox with the precision that had become synonymous with Noah Fischer: Effective immediately, all primary accounts will report directly to the Executive Director's office for review. Yu-won stared at the email, the corner of his mouth twitching in irritation. The office was already buzzing with reactions, whispers floating around about how this would disrupt workflows or delay deadlines. Yu-won, however, wasn’t concerned about the logistics. He knew exactly who this was targeting. He leaned back in his chair, staring at the email on his screen like it might offer some kind of explanation. Then he glanced across the room at Jaehyun, who was busy fiddling with his phone and looking blissfully unbothered. “This guy,” Yu-won muttered under his breath, shaking his head.


By the time 7:00 PM rolled around, Yu-won found himself in the conference room with Noah and the Anderson campaign pitch once again. The room was dim except for the soft glow of the projector, and Yu-won’s nerves were starting to fray after hours of revisions. Noah sat at the head of the table, his sharp gaze fixed on the presentation slides. His silence was as oppressive as it was maddening, the only sound in the room the faint click of his pen. “So, this is the updated pitch,” Yu-won said, keeping his tone steady as he gestured toward the screen. “The visuals, the messaging, and the timeline have all been refined according to your feedback.” Noah’s expression didn’t shift. He simply leaned back in his chair, his pen tapping softly against the table. “It’s an improvement.” Yu-won resisted the urge to roll his eyes. Coming from Noah, that was practically glowing praise. “Good to know my three sleepless nights paid off.” “But,” Noah continued, and Yu-won’s patience immediately frayed, “the tagline could still be stronger. It’s close, but it’s not there yet.” Yu-won inhaled sharply, keeping his temper in check. “And what, exactly, is wrong with the tagline?” Noah’s gaze flicked to him, calm and cutting. “It’s serviceable. Not memorable.” Yu-won opened his mouth to argue, but Noah cut him off. “The Andersons didn’t hire us to deliver serviceable. They hired us because they want to stand out. And that’s what we’ll give them.” The words stung more than they should have. “I see,” Yu-won said stiffly, his hands curling into fists at his sides. “Anything else? Maybe the font could use more ‘emotional depth.’” he said, almost mimicking what Noah had said in his feedback. To his surprise, Noah’s lips twitched, almost imperceptibly, as if suppressing a smile. “The font’s fine,” he said dryly. That almost made Yu-won feel better. Almost. “Is that all, then?” he asked, folding his arms. “Or should I prepare for a fourth revision?” Noah stood, gathering his things with his usual brisk efficiency. “That’ll do for now. Good work.” For a moment, Yu-won just blinked at him. He couldn’t remember the last time Noah had outright complimented him, even in such a clipped, impersonal way. It threw him off balance, and before he could think better of it, he asked, “Wait, that’s it? No scathing critique?” Noah paused by the door, raising an eyebrow. “Don’t push your luck, Yu-won-ssi.” And with that, he was gone, leaving Yu-won staring after him, half-annoyed and half… something else.

The next afternoon, a follow-up email from the Anderson team sent the office into celebration: [We’ve reviewed your latest proposal and are thrilled with the direction. Looking forward to working on the full branding campaign with your team.] The news spread like wildfire. By lunchtime, Jaehyun had already suggested drinks to celebrate, and the marketing team eagerly agreed. Yu-won hadn’t planned on going, but somehow, he got dragged along, wedged between Jaehyun and a junior copywriter, a drink already in his hand. As the evening wore on, the private room at Seoulite filled with laughter and clinking glasses. Empty soju bottles lined the table as the team toasted their success, and even Yu-won allowed himself to relax, his usual sharp tongue softened by the alcohol. “To Yu-won!” Jaehyun declared, raising his glass with a grin. “Our creative genius, even if he does drive Fischer-ssi insane.” The table erupted into laughter. Yu-won groaned, burying his face in his hands. “Can we not?” “Oh, come on,” Jaehyun teased, nudging him. “Don’t pretend you didn’t love all that extra attention.” “Extra attention?” Yu-won snorted, his cheeks turning pink. “He nitpicked the tagline for an hour.” “And it worked,” Jaehyun pointed out. “The client loved it.” Yu-won couldn’t argue with that, but the memory of Noah’s sharp gaze still lingered in his mind. There was something about the way Noah had looked at him last night—cool and calculating, but with an intensity that made his stomach twist. He hated it. Or at least, he told himself he did. He hated how it made his heart flutter... “Drink up!” someone called, pushing another shot toward him. “I really shouldn’t, I'm already kinda—” Yu-won started, but Jaehyun had already filled his glass. “One more won’t kill you,” Jaehyun said, slinging an arm around his shoulders. “We’re celebrating, remember?” "It's just a small thing..." Yu-won tried to protest, but Jaehyun insisted, "A win is a win!"

Across the table, Noah watched the scene unfold, fingers drumming lightly against the rim of his untouched glass. His sharp eyes flicked to Jaehyun’s arm around Yu-won, then to the way Yu-won leaned into the touch just a little too easily. His jaw tightened, but he forced himself to stay seated. This was fine. Yu-won could do whatever he wanted. But then Yu-won let out a loud, delighted laugh at something Jaehyun whispered in his ear, tipping his head back against Jaehyun’s shoulder like he belonged there, and something in Noah snapped. Noah stood abruptly, the legs of his chair scraping against the wooden floor loud enough to cut through the chatter. “That’s enough,” he said coolly, his gaze fixed on Yu-won. “You’re done for the night.” Yu-won blinked up at him, his smile slow and dazed. “Huh?” “You’re drunk,” Noah said matter-of-factly, reaching for his wrist. “I’m not drunk,” Yu-won argued, even as he nearly tripped over his own feet. The faint flush in his cheeks and the slur in his words suggested otherwise. “As your direct supervisor, it’s my responsibility,” Noah said, his tone leaving no room for argument. “Say goodnight, Yu-won-ssi.” Grumbling under his breath, Yu-won let himself be pulled to his feet, swaying slightly. “Bye-bye, everyone,” he muttered, waving half-heartedly. “See you Monday.” In the elevator, Yu-won stayed quiet, his head leaning back against the wall, but a lazy smile played on his lips. When they reached the parking garage, he finally spoke. “You know, you’re kind of a buzzkill,” he mumbled, his voice thick with sleepiness. “And you’re reckless,” Noah replied without missing a beat. “Where do you live?” “It’s a secret,” Yu-won said, grinning lazily. “You’ll just come give me more work.” Noah sighed, already regretting his decision. “My place is closer. You can sleep it off there.” Yu-won didn’t argue, too tired to protest. As Noah helped him into the car, his eyes fluttered shut, his head lolling against the seat. “Noah-ssi…” he murmured, his voice barely audible. “You’re nice sometimes…” Noah didn’t respond, his hands tightening on the steering wheel. He told himself it didn’t mean anything, that he was just doing his job. But as he glanced at Yu-won’s peaceful expression, something dark and dangerous stirred in his chest. _This is going to be a problem, he thought grimly. A very big problem._

The drive back to Noah’s apartment was silent, save for the hum of the engine. Yu-won sat slumped in the passenger seat, his tie loosened and his head tilted back against the headrest. His eyes fluttered open occasionally, catching fleeting glimpses of streetlights casting golden streaks across the car’s interior. “You didn’t have to drag me out like that,” he muttered, his words slurred but coherent enough. “I could’ve handled myself.” “You were on the verge of passing out,” Noah replied, his eyes fixed on the road ahead. His tone was clipped, professional, but the way his fingers gripped the steering wheel betrayed a trace of irritation—or concern. Yu-won let out a quiet laugh, his lips quirking into a lazy smile. “You’re such a control freak, Noah-ssi. Do you ever let go of the wheel? Metaphorically speaking, I mean.” Noah’s hands tightened on the steering wheel. “I let go when I trust the other person not to crash.” “Harsh,” Yu-won muttered, his gaze drifting toward Noah’s profile. For a moment, he seemed to forget whatever teasing remark he was about to make, his brow furrowing slightly. “Your eyes…” Noah glanced at him briefly. “What about them?” “They’re so intense,” Yu-won said softly, his voice almost wistful. “Like… you’re always trying to figure something out. Always working. But when you focus…” He trailed off, his cheeks flushing faintly, though whether it was from the wine or his own thoughts, Noah couldn’t tell. “They’re kind of pretty. Annoyingly so.” Noah’s jaw tightened, the unexpected compliment catching him off guard. He didn’t respond, unsure how to interpret the sudden shift in Yu-won’s tone. “Do you ever stop thinking?” Yu-won asked after a moment, his voice quieter now. “About work, or… whatever it is you’re always brooding about?” Noah pulled into his apartment complex, cutting the engine with a smooth motion. He turned to Yu-won, his expression unreadable. “You’re drunk.” “Maybe,” Yu-won admitted, his smile turning sly. “But drunk people are honest, aren’t they? Isn’t that the whole thing? Truth serum, or whatever?” “You’re rambling,” Noah said, though his voice lacked its usual sharpness. He opened his door and walked around to the passenger side, pulling it open with more force than necessary. “Come on.”

Yu-won let Noah help him out of the car, leaning against him as they made their way to the elevator. His head lolled slightly, resting briefly on Noah’s shoulder before he pulled back with a faint pout. “Noah-ssi,” he murmured, his fingers curling into Noah’s sleeve. “Why do you care so much?” “What?” “About me,” Yu-won said, looking up at him with bleary, half-lidded eyes. “You act like I’m some big inconvenience, but you still—still…” He broke off, biting his lip. “You don’t hate me as much as you pretend, do you?” Noah froze, his breath catching in his throat. “Yu-won, you’re—” “Say it,” Yu-won interrupted, his voice low but insistent. “If you don’t hate me, say it.” Noah hesitated, his usual composure slipping as he met Yu-won’s gaze. The younger man’s expression was open, vulnerable in a way that Noah had never seen before. It made something in his chest twist painfully. “Yu-won,” he said finally, his voice quieter now. “You should sleep this off.” Yu-won tilted his head, studying Noah with a dazed, glassy gaze. His eyes struggled to focus, and the world around him felt distant and blurred, like he was looking through a fogged window. He swayed slightly, catching himself with a hand on Noah’s arm. Then, without warning, he rose onto his tiptoes, brushing his lips clumsily against the corner of Noah’s mouth. It wasn’t smooth or confident—it was fleeting and awkward, but it sent a jolt through both of them. “Thank you,” Yu-won whispered, his voice trembling. His lips lingered near Noah’s cheek for a beat too long before he pulled back, unsteady on his feet. His cheeks flushed, and he dropped his gaze, unable to meet Noah’s eyes. “For taking care of me.” Noah didn’t move, his mind reeling. The kiss had been fleeting, barely more than a touch, but the heat of it lingered, burning against his skin like a brand. He opened his mouth to say something, anything, but the words stuck in his throat. Yu-won stumbled slightly, gripping Noah’s shirt for balance. His breaths were uneven, and his lips parted as if he wanted to speak, but he didn’t. Instead, his unfocused gaze drifted up toward Noah’s face, lingering on his lips. “I can’t…” Yu-won mumbled, his voice barely audible. “I can’t… kiss you properly… I’m too…” He trailed off, shaking his head as if frustrated with himself. His fingers curled tighter into Noah’s shirt. “Noah-ssi… kiss… kiss me properly. Please.” The words hit Noah like a bolt of lightning, shattering his restraint. His chest tightened as he looked down at Yu-won, whose wide eyes brimmed with something raw and vulnerable. In this moment, without his usual sharp defenses, Yu-won looked fragile. He wasn’t teasing or deflecting—he was simply asking.
